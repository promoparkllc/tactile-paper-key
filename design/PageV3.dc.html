01Where it comes from

Object first. Interface second.

We didn’t start in a design tool. We built a desk keypad in Blender, pressed it, lit it, and then flattened it piece by piece until it became the player.

1 · BRIEF
A desk objectKeys that latchA small displayOne glaze
Hi-fi panels and desk instruments, not screens. Matte, quiet, one accent.
2 · THREE DIRECTIONS
Graphite read as a default dark UI; Instrument got busy. Paper Key won.
3 · THE OBJECT Real parts, real numbers: Ø 52 keys, 2 mm travel, a 14×32 fader cap.
4 · THE FLATTEN The engraved ring became a 1.25 px border. The groove became the timeline.
5 · THE SKIN
What ships: the same object, now CSS, over any footage.

▶ The film in the player above is this process, 26 seconds, rendered in Blender.

02Anatomy

Eight parts. One dock.

Every control shows its own state on its face — latched, lit or moved — so you can read the player without opening anything.

PLAY 1.0×0:120:26
1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8
1 · PLAY KEYThe only orange key. Its glyph morphs between play and pause.
2 · DISPLAYState, speed and time in one black pill: PLAY 1.0×, PAUSED, LOADING, END.
3 · SEEK FADERA ribbed cap on an engraved line. Hover for a framed preview of that moment.
4 · MUTESpeaker glyph shows the level in waves; muted shows a cross.
5 · VOLUME FADERA short bone fader. Arrow keys move it in 5 % steps.
6 · CAPTIONSLatches down 1 px and lights an LED bar when on.
7 · SETTINGSSpeed, captions and quality, in a bone panel that opens from the key.
8 · FULL SCREENCorners out to enter, corners in to leave. The dock scales up to 1.25×.
